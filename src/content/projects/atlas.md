LOW-TECH deals with low-technology approaches and how they can redefine contemporary understandings of materiality and design practice within interaction design. The main research problem addresses the overreliance on high-tech paradigms that often obscure the material, experiential, and pedagogical dimensions of design. Simultaneously, the use of lower-cost and less complex technologies and materials can generate more sustainable proposals that promote a critical perspective among designers.

## Approach

We follow a Research-through-Design methodology, combining design processes, material experimentation, pedagogical case studies, and qualitative analysis, grounded by literature review, case analysis, and interviews with design professionals.

## Results

Expected results include comprehensive design guidelines, a digital book with the research synthesis, and a gallery of low-tech interaction design prototypes. All contributing to a critical and sustainable reframing of interaction design’s material and educational foundations.
